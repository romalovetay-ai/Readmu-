# Sumi-e Ink Wash

**Family:** Painterly · **id:** `sumi-e`

**Look.** Monochrome Japanese ink painting on warm, fibrous rice paper with long kozo fibres. Two ridgelines of pale and mid grey wash dissolve into mist, a few small brush-flick birds hang in the sky, and most of the sheet is left empty. A vertical column of calligraphy and a vermilion seal sit at the right edge.

**Motion.** Strokes travel along their path from a wet, solid start into dry, split bristle tracks. Text lands character by character with a soft grey bleed. Mist bands drift slowly across the whole scroll and the calligraphy column is written down from the top.

**How it explains.** Titles and labels are ink-bled Noto Serif JP characters. Arrows are one loaded-brush stroke with a quick flick back from the tip as the head. Boxes are a pale blurred wash card with two confident edge strokes, or an enso circle when asked. Counters are heavy ink numerals. Emphasis is an open vermilion brush circle followed by a pressed red seal. The scene change rolls mist in from the right until the scroll is covered, then thins it away.

**Palette.** ink `#151515`, wash `#6e6c68`, pale `#b4b0a8`, vermilion `#c8321e`, paper `#f2ede0`.

**Fonts.** Noto Serif JP (all lettering, the Japanese calligraphy column and the seal character).

**Signature techniques.**
- `brush()` builds a stroke from a bundle of bristle tracks plus a wet core. Past a dry point, noise opens gaps (more on the outer bristles) so the stroke splits, and ink pools where it touches down.
- Mountain ridgelines come from noise-shaped silhouettes with one or two dominant peaks, filled with a blurred gradient that fades to mist, textured by short dry rubs and re-stated with a dry-brush contour.
- The seal is a red square with speckle cut out by `destination-out`, then a character knocked out of it.
- Mist in the finish is a stretched radial gradient drifting across the frame.

**SFX.** bed `ocean`, `swell` opening chord, `scribble` (brush medium) for every stroke, `pluck` notes as items land (a rising scale for the platform chips), `whoosh` for the mist, `count` for the numerals, `stamp` for the seal, `chime` at the end.

**When to use it.** Calm, minimalist or reflective pieces where breathing room matters: focus, craft, simplicity, "do less" messages, or premium and mindful branding.
