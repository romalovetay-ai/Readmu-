# Halftone Pop Panel

**Family:** Print · **id:** `halftone-pop`

**Look.** A blown-up Lichtenstein-style comic panel. A paper gutter surrounds a white field covered in a blue Ben-Day dot grid, ringed by a fat tapering black brush border. Flat process yellow fills captions and arrows, red dots shade the undersides, and everything is lettered in Bangers. The finish makes it look printed on aged newsprint with a slightly slipped cyan plate.

**Motion.** Words slam in one at a time, starting 1.6 times too big and settling with a small overshoot. Boxes and chips pop in with a back-ease, and arrows grow along their path. The demo adds a camera shake on the emphasis hit.

**How it explains.** Titles sit on a yellow narrator caption box. Labels are white caption chips with brushed borders, or speech balloons with a tail. Arrows are fat yellow comic arrows with a red dot-shaded underside and a black rim. Boxes are flat or dotted panels with a hard offset shadow. The counter bursts out of a yellow explosion balloon, and emphasis is radiating brush speed lines plus a red "WOW!" burst stuck to the corner. The scene change is a slanted new panel that slams across with a "MEANWHILE..." caption and slides off.

**Palette.** paper `#fbf2da`, black `#141414`, yellow `#ffd21f`, red `#e2231a`, blue `#1d5bd0`, skin `#f05a5a`, white `#fffaf0`.

**Fonts.** Bangers, used for every piece of text.

**Signature techniques.**
- Cached staggered Ben-Day dot tiles used as repeating patterns for fields, shading and the burst.
- A tapering brush polygon built from a densified polyline with noise on the width, so outlines swell in the middle and thin at the ends.
- Jagged burst polygons for the counter, the WOW burst and the speech shapes.
- Finish pass copies the frame, tints it cyan and screens it back offset by a couple of pixels, then multiplies aged newsprint, a warm vignette and soft grain.

**SFX.** Bed `room`. Strokes use the `brush` scribble, labels land with `pop`, emphasis uses `stamp`, the transition uses `whoosh`. The demo score adds `thud` on box slams, `count` for the number and a closing `chime`.

**When to use it.** Punchy, loud explainers, hot takes, before-and-after reveals and anything that should read like a comic page with big numbers and sound effects.
